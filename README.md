## The question

An AI valuation rule scores an issuer's disclosure. Investors act on the score, so the
score influences how much capital the firm receives. Capital changes the firm's later
performance — and that performance becomes part of the rule's training history. Part of
the rule's evidence about itself is therefore its own prior decision.

> **Under what condition may such a rule safely revise itself, and does the allocation
> mechanism decide whether that condition can hold?**

**Our answer.** It may revise itself only while its own capital footprint is
*identifiable*. We make the first-stage `F` statistic of an exchange-set supply shock the
governing criterion: below `F = 10` the evaluator is frozen and the last audited rule is
restored; above it, revision resumes automatically.

## Headline results (simulated, 20 seeds, ±95% CI across seeds)

| Mechanism | Memory | mean manipulation | price RMSE | feedback bias | first-stage F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 uniform-price | No memory (committed) | 0.406 ±.00 | 0.45 | 0.000 | 5939 |
| M1 | **Naive history** | **0.824** ±.14 | 8.14 | **1.030** ±.28 | 231 |
| M1 | Capital-purged | 0.455 ±.09 | 5.26 | 0.090 ±.17 | 36 |
| M1 | **Audited freeze** | 0.423 ±.06 | **2.57** | **0.038** ±.14 | 143 |
| M2 bookbuilding | No memory | 0.406 ±.00 | 0.78 | 0.000 | 42537 |
| M2 | Naive history | 0.717 ±.09 | 4.78 | 0.768 ±.18 | 1814 |
| M2 | Capital-purged | 0.395 ±.05 | 1.59 | −0.024 ±.09 | 2082 |
| M2 | Audited freeze | 0.416 ±.03 | 1.28 | 0.016 ±.06 | 5275 |

Generated by `src/run_experiment.py` into [`results/headline_table.csv`](results/headline_table.csv).
**All data are synthetic.** No claim is made about any real issuer.

1. **Outcome-based revision roughly doubles strategic disclosure** (0.406 → 0.824, M1).
2. **It is the capital channel, not the synthetic setup.** Setting ρ = 0 removes the
   effect entirely (+0.328 → −0.019). The sign holds in **27/27** parameter settings, and purging reduces |bias| in all 27.
3. **Purging works — but only when identified.** Bias falls 1.030 → 0.090.
4. **Below F = 10 the correction is unstable**, swinging to −2.563. The audited rule's
   worst case over the same sweep is **0.275**, and it unfreezes itself (100% → 0%) as
   identification arrives.
5. **The audit rule must earn the right to certify.** Without a 40-issuer guard it
   certifies on noise and locks in permanently.
6. **Feedback degrades rationing.** Once the offering is genuinely scarce, the inflated
   signal compresses bids: M1 excludes 9.5 investors under naive history vs 17.2 under
   commitment. Retail share rises for a bad reason — the auction stopped sorting.
7. **Honest negative.** Capital–ρ correlation stays ≤ 0.021 in all 16 cells: nobody observes ρ,
   so no mechanism here achieves allocative efficiency on it.
8. **The safeguard is not free — and we only saw this when challenged.** Team FP4's Oct 12
   duet selection of Milli et al. (2019) predicts that strategy-robustness buys
   institutional utility at a matching *social burden* on those classified. Tested on our
   own run: versus the unsafeguarded rule, the audited evaluator serves **13.1 fewer of 35
   retail investors** (99.9% → 62.5%) and **4.2 more of 15 institutions** (36.7% → 64.8%).
   The whole cost of restoring the evaluator's integrity falls on the least-informed
   participants. We no longer claim the audited rule is an improvement full stop — it is an
   integrity gain with a named distributional price.

### Two defects we found and fixed in our own PS2 code

1. **First-stage `F` was mis-normalised** — divided by the residual *sum of squares*
   instead of the residual *variance*, understating every reported value by a factor of
   `n − 2`. Our PS2 paper reported **0.136**; recomputed correctly it is **5.47**.
   The legacy formula is retained as `Evaluator.first_stage_F_legacy()` so the correction
   is auditable rather than silently applied.
2. **The purge read `b_true`** when constructing its own target — it used the answer it
   was meant to recover. Replaced with textbook 2SLS, verified by running the estimator
   under `b_true = 1.02` and `5.00` and obtaining identical output to nine decimals.

This reversed our PS2 adverse finding, and the reason is identification: under the PS2
mechanism the correction was estimated on variation that barely existed.
